Research

Job Market Paper

2026

Market Access, Endogenous Wages, and the Spatial Incidence of Commodity Taxes

Single-authored job market paper.

In this paper I ask wether countries gain from taxing differentiated goods where they are consumed rather than where they are produced. In my two-country model, firms can move , exports are costly, and both skilled and unskilled wages respond to local production. I show that a common tax gives the same real allocation under either principle, although it assigns tax revenue differently. When countries choose their own taxes, the welfare ranking depends on trade costs and on how strongly wages raise the cost of attracting firms. A strong local cost response favors origin taxation at every finite trade cost. With a weaker response, destination taxation can prevail near free trade and again near prohibitive trade, while origin taxation wins in between. I derive the Nash taxes and location responses behind these switches. A larger consumer market can host fewer firms than its expenditure share, and a national welfare gain need not benefit unskilled workers when skilled residents own the local fixed factor.
