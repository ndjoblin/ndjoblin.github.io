Research

Job Market Paper

2026

Commodity Tax Principles, Firm Location, and Welfare Incidence with Trade Costs

Single-authored job market paper.

This paper compares destination and origin commodity taxation in a two-country economy with trade costs, mobile capital, and endogenous skilled and unskilled wages. At a common tax rate, the two principles yield the same firm allocation and world welfare, but different national welfare because they assign tax revenue to different countries. With identical technologies, changing the common rate does not move firms. We then derive firm-location responses and symmetric Nash taxes under both principles. Their welfare ranking depends on which Nash tax comes closer to the subsidy that corrects the monopoly markup. With costless trade, the ranking is governed by how strongly local production costs respond to firm relocation. Moderate trade costs give destination taxes a location effect, allowing origin taxation to outperform destination even where free trade favors destination. Firm attraction raise both wages and widen the skilled-to-unskilled wage ratio. Direction-specific trade frictions preserve the common-tax result but change the location and national incidence of Nash policies.
